Server console rescue under a PayPal lock

No hold, no keystrokes.
Only your server answering can charge you.

When a Linux server stops answering SSH, the last way in is the hosting provider's web console: a screen and a keyboard. Keyhold lends that keyboard to an AI agent. A PayPal authorization is the lock on it, and only your own check of the server can turn the hold into a charge.

How a rescue runs

  1. 1

    Free look

    The agent sees screenshots and cannot press a key. You get the fault, the screen lines that show it and a fixed price: $9 for one config fix, $19 for a boot-path repair, $29 for kernel, initramfs or disk.

  2. 2

    Your probe, fixed at checkout

    An SSH login with a key only the probe holds, and a page that must answer with the content you expect. Its hash goes into the PayPal order.

  3. 3

    Hold

    You approve a PayPal one-time payment. Keyhold creates the order with intent AUTHORIZE for the quote and authorizes it. Nothing is captured.

  4. 4

    The lock

    Every keystroke passes a gate that asks PayPal whether the authorization is still CREATED. Press Stop and the hold is voided and the keyboard is gone.

  5. 5

    Capture on your probe only

    Two passing probes, far enough apart, with no failure between. Then a capture of the quote or less. The agent has no tool that can charge you.

  6. 6

    Void, or refund later

    Giving up, a time limit, Stop, or a probe that never passes voids the hold. If the server falls over twice in a row during the warranty, the capture is refunded in full.
